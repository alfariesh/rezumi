The editor is where you write and shape a resume. This page explains what each part of it does, how your work is saved, and how to lock a finished resume so it doesn't change by accident.

The editor has a rail, a framed area with the panel and the page, and one header row across the top whose parts sit over the sections they belong to:

- **The rail** down the left edge of the window: the back arrow at the top, the modes, and **History** and **AI** at the bottom.
- **The panel**, white, on the left of the frame. The header above it holds the document name; the panel shows whatever you select in the rail.
- **The page canvas**, gray, on the right. The header above it holds undo, redo, zoom and the page count, with **AI** at its right end. The page shows your resume exactly as it will print and updates as you type. **Download PDF** floats over its lower right corner.
- **The assistant**, while it's open, in a column of its own at the right, its header in the same row.

To leave the editor, select the back arrow (**Back to documents**) at the top of the rail. Any unsaved changes are saved first.

## The headers

From left to right, across the header over the panel and the header over the canvas:

| Part                        | What it does                                                                                                                         |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Document name               | Select it to rename the resume in place. Press <kbd>Enter</kbd> to save the name or <kbd>Esc</kbd> to keep the old one.              |
| Arrow next to the name      | Opens the document menu (see below). The cloud icon after it is the save status.                                                     |
| Undo and redo (arrow icons) | Undo or redo your last change. <kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>Z</kbd> and <kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>⇧</kbd> <kbd>Z</kbd> do the same outside text fields. |
| Zoom and page count         | Zoom the page and fit it to the canvas. See [Zooming](#zooming).                                                                     |

**Download PDF** isn't in a header. It floats over the lower right corner of the page and downloads the resume as a PDF straight away. The arrow beside it (**More download formats**) opens the **Download** sheet with every other format. **History** opens from the rail, and the AI assistant from the rail or the **AI** icon at the right end of the canvas header; see [AI and History](#ai-and-history).

On a phone a single bar replaces the headers. It keeps the back arrow, the name, the menu arrow and the save status, plus a **Download PDF** icon. See [Editing on a phone or tablet](/guides/editing-on-mobile).

## The four modes

The modes run down the rail at the left edge of the window; select one to change what the panel shows. **Check** shows how many issues are open, or a check mark when there are none. **Share** shows a dot when the resume is public. The page stays in view in every mode.

- **Write** is for content: your name and contact details, the summary, and every section and entry. See [Filling in your details](/guides/filling-in-your-details) and [Managing sections](/guides/managing-sections).
- **Design** is for the look of the page: template, fonts, colors, page format and layout. A row of chips at the top of the panel jumps to each group, and marks the one you're looking at as you scroll. See [Choosing a template](/guides/choosing-a-template).
- **Check** reviews the resume for problems, and can compare it with a job posting. See [Checking your resume](/guides/checking-your-resume).
- **Share** publishes the resume as a web page and sets its address and privacy. The page area previews the web page instead of the PDF. See [Sharing your resume as a web page](/guides/sharing-your-resume-publicly).

When you're not typing in a field, press <kbd>1</kbd>, <kbd>2</kbd>, <kbd>3</kbd> or <kbd>4</kbd> to switch to Write, Design, Check or Share. The mode is part of the page address, so reloading the page or opening a bookmark keeps you in the same mode.

### AI and History

At the bottom of the rail are **History** and **AI**.

- **AI** opens the AI assistant in a section of its own at the right, beside the page (below 1280 pixels wide it slides over the page instead). The **AI** icon at the right end of the canvas header does the same. You can also press <kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>J</kbd> (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd> <kbd>J</kbd>) to open or close it. See [Using the assistant](/guides/using-the-assistant).
- **History** shows the resume's version history inside the panel. A version you pick shows read-only on the page; leaving History returns the page to now. See [Undoing changes and version history](/guides/undoing-changes-and-version-history).

## The page canvas

The page on the right is a live preview of the PDF you'll download. Each page has a label above it, such as **Page 1**.

- **Click a line on the page to edit it.** Hovering over the page tints the block under the pointer. Clicking it selects that entry, marks it with an **Editing** tag, and opens it in the Write panel. Clicking your name or contact details opens the Basics card. Press <kbd>Esc</kbd> to clear the selection.
- **Proposed edits** from the assistant or from Check appear on the page, with old text struck through and new text highlighted. Nothing changes until you accept them.
- **Overflow warning.** If your content runs onto an extra page, the label above the first page says so, for example "Runs onto page 2 by about 6 lines", and offers **Fit to one page**. See [Fitting content on a page](/guides/fitting-content-on-a-page).

### Zooming

The zoom controls sit in the canvas header, above the page. (The picture below shows the earlier floating version.)

<Frame caption="The zoom bar">
  <img

    alt="The zoom bar with a minus button, the Fit button, a plus button and the page count"
  />

- Select **−** or **+** to zoom out or in, in 10% steps from 60% to 150%.
- Select the middle button to fit the page to the width of the canvas. It reads **Fit** when the page is fitted, and the zoom level otherwise. You can also press <kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>0</kbd> (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd> <kbd>0</kbd> on Windows and Linux).
- The number on the right is how many pages the resume has.

Zoom only changes what you see in the editor. It has no effect on the PDF or any other download.

## How your work is saved

Rezumi saves as you type. There is no save button. Changes are sent about half a second after you stop typing, and the save status (the cloud icon after the document name) tells you where things stand:

| Status                             | Meaning                                                                                              |
| ---------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Saved**                          | Everything you see is saved to your account.                                                         |
| **Saving…**                        | Your latest change is on its way.                                                                    |
| **Offline · saved on this device** | You've lost your connection. Your changes are kept in this browser and sent when you're back online. |
| **Not saved · Retry**              | The server couldn't save your changes. They're kept in this browser. Select **Retry** to try again.  |

<Frame caption="The editor while offline">
  <img
    src="/images/guides/editor-overview/offline-save-status.webp"
    alt="The save status reading Offline, saved on this device, above a notice that says you can keep editing and that Download and Share need a connection"
  />
</Frame>

A few things to know:

- **You can keep editing offline.** A notice at the top of the panel explains that changes sync when you're back online. **Share** and **Download PDF** are unavailable until then.
- **Unsaved changes survive a reload or a closed tab.** If you come back to a resume whose last changes never reached the server, the editor restores them and says "Restored changes that hadn't been saved yet."
- **Leaving the editor waits for the save.** If saving takes more than about 10 seconds, you stay in the editor and see a message that your changes are still open.
- Pressing <kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>S</kbd> (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd> <kbd>S</kbd>) only reminds you that changes are saved automatically.

Each visit in which you make changes also leaves an autosave version in History, so you can go back to how the resume looked before. Within a visit, press <kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>Z</kbd> (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd> <kbd>Z</kbd>) while you're not typing in a field to undo your last change, and <kbd>⇧</kbd> <kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>Z</kbd> (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd> <kbd>Y</kbd>) to redo it, or use **Undo** and **Redo** in the middle of the editor bar. See [Undoing changes and version history](/guides/undoing-changes-and-version-history).

### Editing in more than one place

If the same resume changes somewhere else while you have it open (in another browser tab, on another device, or through an AI agent or app connected to your account), the editor picks up the change on its own and shows a short message: "Synced changes made in another tab." or "This resume was updated by an AI agent."

- If you're typing in a field at that moment, the update waits until you leave the field, so your typing isn't interrupted.
- If you have changes of your own that haven't been saved yet, your changes win.
- After an outside change, <kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>Z</kbd> can't undo past it. Use History to go further back.

## Locking a resume

Lock a resume when it's finished, or when you've sent it for an application and want to keep it exactly as it was. You can still view, download, print and duplicate a locked resume, and a public link you already made keeps working, but the content and design can't change.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the document menu">Select the arrow next to the document name in the panel header.</Step>
  <Step title="Select Lock editing">
    The resume locks straight away. There's no confirmation, because unlocking is one click. A lock icon appears next to
    the document name.
  </Step>
</Steps>

While a resume is locked:

- The Write panel shows **Locked. Unlock to edit.** and every field is read-only.
- The Design panel is read-only.
- **Rename…** and **Move to Trash** are unavailable in the document menu.
- Share link settings can't be changed, and History can't restore an older version over it.

<Frame caption="A locked resume">
  <img
    src="/images/guides/editor-overview/locked-document.webp"
    alt="The document name with a lock icon, and a notice in the panel reading Locked. Unlock to edit, with an Unlock button"
  />
</Frame>

To edit it again, select **Unlock** in the notice, or choose **Unlock editing** from the document menu.

You can also lock and unlock resumes from the Documents page. See [Managing documents](/guides/managing-documents).
