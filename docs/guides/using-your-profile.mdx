---
title: "Keep your details in one Profile"
description: "Keep your details, photo, summary and career history in one Profile. Resumes, cover letters and your portfolio take them from there, and each resume can still change any part for itself."
---

Your **Profile** holds the details and career history you would otherwise type into every resume. Correct a phone number or a job title there once, and every resume that still uses it follows. A resume can still change anything for itself.

## What the Profile holds

Your Profile holds:

- your details: name, headline, email, phone, location, website and custom fields;
- your photo;
- your summary;
- your entries in every built-in section: experience, education, projects, skills, languages, certifications, awards, publications, volunteering, interests, profiles and references.

Each document keeps its own custom sections, section titles, layout and design (including how the photo looks), and what it hides from the page.

The Profile also keeps the **Facts**, **Stories** and **Preferences** that the coach and the application workspace use. See [Tell your story once](/guides/building-career-knowledge).

## Open your Profile

Select **Profile** in the sidebar. It has four tabs:

- **Details & career** opens first. It has your details, photo, summary and entries, with the same fields as the **Write** panel in the resume editor. Changes save as you type.
- **Facts**, **Stories** and **Preferences** hold your career facts, five-part stories and job-search preferences.

The Profile's address is `/dashboard/profile`. Older links to Knowledge open it too.

## Fill in your Profile

A new Profile starts with the name and email you signed up with. The first time you sign in, a short setup offers to fill the rest from what you already have. See [Setting up your Profile](/guides/setting-up-your-profile). Later, add the rest whichever way suits you:

- On **Details & career**, while the Profile has no career entries yet:
  - **Bring what you have** reads your LinkedIn profile link, a resume file (PDF, Word, JSON or a LinkedIn data export), your GitHub username or your personal website, the same way as the first-time setup. You check what was found, untick what you'd rather leave out, then select **Add**. You can undo it like any other edit.
  - **Fill from a resume…** fills your Profile from one of your resumes.
- **New → Import a resume** creates a resume from a file and also fills your Profile. See [Importing a resume](/guides/importing-resumes).
- Type directly on **Details & career**.
- Use **Save to Profile** in a resume you are editing, one part at a time. See [Edit inside a resume](#edit-inside-a-resume).

Filling only adds what your Profile lacks: blank details, a missing photo or summary, and entries for sections that have none yet. Nothing already in your Profile changes. Sample resumes never fill your Profile.

## How resumes follow your Profile

Every resume keeps its own copy of its content. Each part of it follows your Profile on its own: a detail such as your phone number, the photo, the summary, or a single entry. A part follows while it still reads exactly like the Profile's version.

When you change your Profile:

- Every resume part that still matches the Profile changes with it.
- A part a resume changed for itself stays as it is.
- Locked resumes, resumes in Trash and the versions recorded as sent to an employer never change.
- New entries are not added to your existing resumes. Add them with **Add from Profile** in each resume.
- An entry you remove from the Profile stays in the resumes that use it.

After a save that other resumes followed, **Details & career** says how many, for example "Updated in 3 resumes". A resume that is open in the editor at the time shows "Updated from your Profile."

For example, you correct your phone number in the Profile. Three resumes still show the old number, so all three change. A fourth resume, where you had typed a different number, keeps its own.

## Edit inside a resume

Editing a resume changes only that resume. When a part differs from your Profile, a small marker appears with two actions:

- **Use Profile's** replaces the resume's version with the Profile's. You can undo it like any other edit.
- **Save to Profile** makes this resume's version the Profile's. Other resumes still following that part change too.

A field you leave empty in a resume gets no marker and never fills itself from your Profile; a small **Use Profile's** next to it fills it in when you want. A resume that has nothing in common with your Profile, such as a sample, shows no markers at all.

An entry your Profile doesn't have gets no marker. To add it to your Profile, select **Save to Profile** in the entry's **⋯** menu.

Each built-in section offers **Add from Profile** next to its add button, which lists the Profile entries this resume doesn't have yet. **Add section** also lists, under **From your Profile**, the sections this resume doesn't use yet that your Profile has entries for; choosing one brings them all.

## New documents start from it

- **Start blank** creates a resume with all your Profile's details and entries. **Try a sample** stays a fictional example. See [Creating your first resume](/guides/creating-your-first-resume).
- A cover letter's **From** section takes your details from your **Profile** unless you choose a resume or the letter's own details. See [Writing a cover letter](/guides/writing-a-cover-letter#fill-in-the-letter).
- A portfolio can use your **Profile** as its source, so its page shows your Profile's details and career as they are now. See [Creating a portfolio website](/guides/creating-a-portfolio).

## Profile and account details

Your Profile is separate from **Account details** in **Settings → Account**: the name, username, email and photo of the account you sign in with. The username is also part of your public links. Changing your account name or photo does not change your resumes. See [Updating your account details](/guides/updating-your-profile).

## Your data

[Exporting your data](/guides/exporting-your-data) includes your Profile as `profile.json`. Facts, stories and preferences are in `career.json`.
